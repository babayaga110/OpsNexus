01The core loop in one picture

Everything in the platform serves one cycle. Headquarters defines the standard, branches execute it with proof, failures turn into tracked fixes, and management sees the result.

STEP 1 · HQConfigure

Admins build checklists, link them to SFDA, Balady or brand rules, and set schedules, evidence rules, scoring and SLAs. No developer needed.

STEP 2 · BRANCHExecute

Staff complete checklists on mobile, inside the branch geofence, with photos, readings, timestamps and a signature.

STEP 3 · SYSTEMDetect & fix

A failed item forces evidence, opens a corrective action with an owner and SLA, and escalates until it is verified and closed.

STEP 4 · MANAGEMENTSee & improve

Scores roll up from department to group. Repeat failures show up, and later the AI layer predicts risk.

What comes out of step 4 (a new SOP, a changed threshold, a new checklist item) feeds back into step 1.

02Modules: one engine, add-ons on top

The BRD's main instruction to developers: do not build fixed screens per checklist. The checklist engine and compliance library are the foundation. Every other module plugs into them and can be sold separately.

03How a hospitality group is organised in the system

Each checklist, score and permission sits somewhere in an eight-level tree. Dashboards drill down the same path, and a role's scope is a cut through it.

04The eight roles and who reports to whom

Each role's scope widens as you move up. Work is done at the bottom and checked one level up, and visibility collects at the top. The Auditor sits outside the chain with read-only access.

05Who can do what

The role-based access matrix as listed in section 6. A filled dot means a primary duty and a hollow dot means read-only access.

06A day at Riyadh 01, lane by lane

A swimlane view of one ordinary day with one failure. It shows which role touches the work at each moment, and how the system steps in automatically.

07What happens when a checklist item fails

The decision logic behind sections 12, 33, 43 and 67. The checklist cannot be submitted until the mandatory fields are filled, and a corrective action cannot close without verification.

08The life of a task

Every task, whether created by hand or automatically from a failed item, passes through the same six statuses in section 30. Verification sends weak work back to be redone.

09Escalation: how fast problems climb

Each issue gets a priority, and the priority sets how long it can stay open before it goes to the next level. All thresholds are configurable (section 34).

10The Saudi compliance library: why each item exists

The BRD calls this the main differentiator. Regulations live as versioned data, not code, and each checklist answer can be traced back to the rule that requires it.

11How a compliance score is calculated

The basic score is completed items ÷ applicable items × 100. The engine also weights categories, so a food-safety miss costs more than a customer-service miss.

12Certificate and licence expiry alerts

Health certificates, food-safety and first-aid certificates, Balady and tourism licences, and contractor documents all sit in one register with automatic countdown alerts.

13Working offline in a basement kitchen

Cold rooms and back-of-house areas often have no signal. The app keeps working and syncs later without creating duplicates (section 49).

14Recommended architecture

The technical stack from section 62. Requests flow down from the apps to storage, and integrations connect at the API.
